### 🔴 The hazard, quoted verbatim

> *"If you send us a pitch that is plagiarized or A.I.-generated, your email address will be
> blacklisted and blocked."*

**Read that as written: the penalty is permanent and it attaches to the email address, not the
pitch.** It is the single most consequential sentence found in this research and it governs how any
draft produced with my help may be used. See "How the drafting has to work" below.

### Sequencing consequence

AARP wants *links to recent writing samples* and *rarely uses unsolicited ideas*. With no byline yet,
a cold AARP pitch is close to a guaranteed no. **AARP is a second-round target** — worth far more
once caregiver.com has published something to point at. Pitching it first spends the best outlet on
the weakest version of the approach.

## How the drafting has to work

The AARP sentence changes the shape of this, and it is worth taking seriously across all four
outlets rather than only the one that says it out loud.

**An AI-drafted piece submitted as-is is a real risk to the sender's address, permanently.** So the
draft I produce is **raw material, not a submission**: the structure, the argument, the specific
facts about the product, and the compliance constraints in one place — for Steve to rewrite in his
own voice. That was already the stronger option for a personal op-ed; it is now also the safe one.

The parts I can supply without that risk:
- the outlet-by-outlet constraints above
- the argument's spine and the facts it must not get wrong
- the boilerplate and author line (`lib/g1/press-kit.ts`), which are factual rather than authored
- a compliance read of a draft Steve writes — §1a third person, no estate, no medical claims
