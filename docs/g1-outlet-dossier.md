## 🔴 caregiver.com — *Today's Caregiver* — OPEN, and the best first target

The outlet Steve named, and the one with the clearest open door.

| | |
|---|---|
| Word count | *"We are looking for articles between 500 and 1500 words depending on the subject."* |
| How to submit | *"Please send us your articles and poems in a word document as an attachment to your email."* — to **nancy@caregiver.com** |
| Topics | includes **practical solutions**, **eldercare** and **preparedness** — angle 3 sits squarely in all three |
| Bio | *"Please include a short bio about the author."* |
| Payment | *"No fees will be paid for any submissions for publication unless specific arrangements are made in writing."* |
| Rights | they send a release form on acceptance; the author retains copyright |

⚠️ **The guidelines say nothing either way about contributors who run a business in the space, and
nothing about promotional content.** That silence is not permission — it means the judgement is
theirs, made on reading the piece. Be conservative: problem-first, product in the bio, and disclose
the interest.

**Note the wordcount is the constraint that shapes the piece: 500–1500 words.**

## DailyCaring — open, but the route could not be verified

Its guidelines are the most useful of any outlet here **and I could not confirm them from a live
page.** The content below comes from a search index; the two obvious URLs are dead:
`/partner-with-us-dailycaring` now redirects to Contact Us, and `/write-for-us/` returns 404.

Reported rules — **treat as unverified**:

- *"Guest articles must NOT be advertisements for your company, product, or service"*
- written by established experts in their field
- practical advice for families caring for older adults
- a brief bio with a link back, **50 words or less**, at the end
- submit to **guest@dailycaring.com**, subject line *"DailyCaring guest article"*
- distributed in their daily email newsletter as well as the site

⚠️ **Do not email `guest@dailycaring.com` on the strength of this file.** Confirm it via their
Contact Us form first. An email to a dead address is a silently lost pitch.

**Even unverified, the advertisement rule is the most useful sentence in this dossier** — it states
plainly the standard every outlet here is applying silently.

## AARP The Magazine — open, high bar, and one hard hazard

| | |
|---|---|
| How to pitch | plain text in the body of an email to **pubspitches@aarp.org** — *"No email attachments"*. Postal address also given |
| Length | *"Maximum one page"*, with *"links to recent writing samples"* |
| Unsolicited | *"The magazine rarely uses unsolicited ideas."* |
| Geography | *"We do not consider pitches from writers based outside of the United States and its territories."* — fine |

### 🔴 The hazard, quoted verbatim

> *"If you send us a pitch that is plagiarized or A.I.-generated, your email address will be
> blacklisted and blocked."*

**Read that as written: the penalty is permanent and it attaches to the email address, not the
pitch.** It is the single most consequential sentence found in this research and it governs how any
draft produced with my help may be used. See "How the drafting has to work" below.

### Sequencing consequence

AARP wants *links to recent writing samples* and *rarely uses unsolicited ideas*. With no byline yet,
a cold AARP pitch is close to a guaranteed no. **AARP is a second-round target** — worth far more
once caregiver.com has published something to point at. Pitching it first spends the best outlet on
the weakest version of the approach.

## How the drafting has to work

The AARP sentence changes the shape of this, and it is worth taking seriously across all four
outlets rather than only the one that says it out loud.

**An AI-drafted piece submitted as-is is a real risk to the sender's address, permanently.** So the
draft I produce is **raw material, not a submission**: the structure, the argument, the specific
facts about the product, and the compliance constraints in one place — for Steve to rewrite in his
own voice. That was already the stronger option for a personal op-ed; it is now also the safe one.

The parts I can supply without that risk:
- the outlet-by-outlet constraints above
- the argument's spine and the facts it must not get wrong
- the boilerplate and author line (`lib/g1/press-kit.ts`), which are factual rather than authored
- a compliance read of a draft Steve writes — §1a third person, no estate, no medical claims
