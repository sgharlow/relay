## 🔴 Read this before the list: the gate's own categories are aimed at the wrong product

`g3-b2b2c-pilot-loi` names *"a benefits broker, credit union, employer benefits provider or wealth
manager"*. Three of those four are **financial** institutions, and that list is inherited from a
version of Relay that had an estate product in it. **Estate was narrowed out PERMANENTLY on
2026-08-14** (`gates.g2-counsel-opinion.declined`) — there is no inheritance feature to sell, and a
wealth manager's interest in this category is almost entirely the inheritance moment.

What is actually left is a **caregiving** product: a parent in rehab for eight weeks, a family that
needs to reach one email account, and an owner who wants it to end when they recover.

The organisations best aligned to that are not financial. They are the companies and coalitions
whose **entire existing job** is sitting with a family in that exact week. Two of the gate's four
categories survive contact with the narrowed product; the other two are ranked below on their
merits, honestly, rather than dropped because I disagree with them.

**This does not change the gate.** It is a recommendation about where to spend the first two
conversations, and the gate's kill condition still measures *meetings taken*, not category.

## 1. 🔴 Homethrive — approach first

A caregiving-support benefit sold to employers, in the same category as Wellthy and Cariloop.

**Why it is first, and it is not the product — it is the behaviour.** Homethrive has publicly done
exactly the thing G3 asks a partner to do, twice in 2026:

| Date | Partnership | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Feb 2026 | **Healthee** | employers add Homethrive's caregiving services inside the Healthee benefits platform |
| May 2026 | **Bright Horizons** | Homethrive **powers** Bright Horizons' new *Care Advising* solution |

A company that has integrated another vendor's capability into its own offering twice this year has
a functioning process for the conversation, and a demonstrated answer of yes. Every other candidate
here is a guess about receptiveness; this one has evidence.

**Fit with the narrowed product.** Their care coordinators already sit with a family in the week
Relay is for. Relay is not a competitor to care navigation — it is the artefact a coordinator could
leave behind: which accounts matter, who can reach them, and how it ends when the parent recovers.

**Route** — ⚠️ **a sales route, not a partner intake.** No partner-program page was found. Expect to
arrive through a demo/contact form and ask to be redirected to partnerships. Say what you want in
the first sentence, because the form is read by people whose job is selling to employers.

## 2. Wellthy — approach second

Caregiver navigation for employers **and health plans** — the health-plan channel is a second
distribution surface the others emphasise less.

**Verified from their own navigation:** `for-employers`, `for-health-plans`, `for-families`. Client
logos named on the homepage include Best Buy, Cisco, Meta, Salesforce, Workday, Hilton, Medtronic
and Merck, under *"Trusted by the world's leading companies and organizations to help their
employees and members access care"*.

**Route** — ⚠️ **`wellthy.com/partners` 404s.** There is no partner page. The only route found is
**`wellthy.com/request-an-employer-demo`**, which is a sales funnel. Same caveat as Homethrive, and
one reason both rank behind the coalition on *cost of the attempt*.

## 3. National Alliance for Caregiving — Innovation Collaborative — run in parallel, it is the cheap one

**A membership coalition of 50+ national healthcare companies, nonprofits, care innovators,
caregiving advocacy organisations and financial-services leaders.** The Innovation Collaborative is
*"a network of national leaders, experts, and influencers working together to identify, strategize
and address complex issues of caregiving across the lifespan."*

**Why it matters even though it is not distribution.** G3 and G1 are separate measurements and this
sits between them: it is the **standing** that gets #1 and #2 to take the meeting, in the same way
the editorial lane's published byline is meant to. A cold form to a benefits vendor from an unknown
one-person product is a different message after a coalition names you.

**Route — the best of any organisation here.** A named human: **`laurenrachel@caregiving.org`**
("Get Involved"), plus a general contact form, `info@caregiving.org`, and 202-918-1013.

⚠️ **Not established:** whether startups/product companies can join, eligibility, and **whether
membership is paid.** The page does not say. Ask that in the first email — if it is a paid tier it
belongs with CAN below, under the same rule.

## 6. ❌ Caregiver Action Network — Corporate Leadership Council — NOT NOW, and the reason is a rule

CAN is one of the best-known caregiver organisations, and this is the wrong instrument.

**It is a PAID, TIERED SPONSORSHIP.** Verified from their own page: partner logos are grouped
Sustaining / Champion / Leading / Caring / Supporting, the invitation is *"We invite your company to
join the Corporate Leadership Council"*, and the stated benefits are *"ongoing marketing exposure to
family caregivers"* and access to experts. Pricing is not published; you email
**`cbrowning@caregiveraction.org`** to find out.

**Why not now.** The global rule is *demand gate before horizontal build* — one arms-length demand
signal before marketing spend. `demand_signal: none`, `g1-arms-length-demand` is at 0, and the
portfolio has cleared arms-length revenue zero times in ~24 months. **Paying a caregiver nonprofit
for marketing exposure before one stranger has said they would pay is the exact pattern that rule
exists to stop** — and it is the same reasoning that declined a $5–15k counsel opinion on
2026-08-14.

**Revisit when** G1 produces its first arms-length person, or a Tier-1 conversation makes CAN
membership a condition of proceeding. Note CAN also runs **CARE2030**, described as connecting
*"caregivers, organizations, employers, innovators and key stakeholders"* — worth one question in
the same email, because it may be a non-paid route into the same room.

## 7–9. The gate's remaining categories, ranked honestly

**7. Benefits brokers.** Real, and the gate names them. But the platforms in #1–#4 are the
*vendors brokers place* — going through them puts Relay one step closer to the member and skips a
sales cycle. Approach brokers directly only if #1–#4 all decline, and then via a specific named
firm, not the category.

**8. Credit unions.** A plausible member-benefit route and genuinely aligned in spirit — a
member-owned institution whose members are ageing. ⚠️ **Unverified: no credit-union or CUSO
partner route was checked for this dossier.** Procurement is slow and typically annual. Do the
research before spending a conversation.

**9. Wealth managers.** ⚠️ **Weakest fit of the nine, and the gate should probably drop them.** The
wealth-management interest in this category is the inheritance/estate moment, and estate was
narrowed out of the product permanently. Approaching one means either mis-selling a feature that
does not exist or leading with the caregiving story to an audience organised around the other one.

## What does not count, restated where it will be read

From `g3-b2b2c-pilot-loi.what_does_not_count`, because the temptation lands hardest during
outreach: a friendly introductory call with no discussion of putting Relay in front of their
people; an advisor, mentor or investor; a partner who wants the estate product. **And nothing
routed through the day job, in either direction** — the employer-anonymity rule applies with full
force.

Also, from the same block: **a pilot signature does not count toward `g1-arms-length-demand`.** That
gate wants a *person* who pays or writes that they would. If a pilot later produces such a person,
they count there, on their own evidence.

## The unanswered question every one of these will ask

`g2-counsel-opinion` was **declined, not satisfied**, so Relay goes into every one of these
conversations from *"no legal opinion exists"*. A regulated partner's diligence asks the questions
that opinion would have answered — `docs/g2-counsel-brief.md` Q1, Q3, Q11 — and *"we did not look"*
is a worse answer than an adverse one.

That is **not** a reason to delay: `g2`'s own `revisit:` trigger (a) is precisely this conversation
happening, at which point counsel gets funded out of a real opportunity instead of ahead of one.
Go in expecting the question and knowing the answer is "that is what this pilot would fund."
