UC-01 — Know whether the vault could actually open

ActorOwner
GoalFind out, without expertise, whether the plan would work in an emergency.
StepsSign in → land on the vault.
ExpectedA standing statement of how prepared the vault is, on every owner screen, naming what is missing rather than only a colour.
VerifiedPass. Banner states reachability in plain words; items ranked by consequence; root credentials flagged as the reset path for everything else.

UC-02 — Build the circle

ActorOwner
GoalName the people who would step in, and make them able to act.
Steps
  1. Add a person, choose how their code reaches them (read aloud or emailed).
  2. After they accept, call them and compare a four-word phrase.
  3. Confirm the match — or reject it, which severs the binding and kills the ticket.
  4. For somebody who will never hold an account, record that they are covered by paper only.
  5. Issue an emergency code as a way back in.
ExpectedEach light says what is missing, not just a state; naming somebody is visibly only half the job; an excluded person stops being nagged about but still counts against the arithmetic.
VerifiedPass. Lights read “Verified — their answer counts”, “Accepted — not yet verified, so their answer would not count”, “Has not accepted yet — give them their code”, “Emergency code only”. The fallback line names anybody who could not get back in on a new device.

UC-03 — Decide who can reach what

ActorOwner
GoalGrant scoped access, tied to a trigger, without granting everything.
ExpectedRules are per item, per person, per trigger. Estate is refused at the API, not merely hidden in a dropdown, until counsel clears it.
VerifiedPass. USER_SELECTABLE_TRIGGER_TYPES is enforced at four separate trust boundaries: /api/rules, /api/policies, /api/triggers/[id]/config, /api/triggers/[id]/initiate.

UC-04 — Configure and run a release

ActorOwner
GoalSet how many confirmations a release needs, and be able to stop it.
ExpectedA threshold that nobody could satisfy is refused when it is set, rather than discovered as a release that silently never completes. Emergencies are reversible; standing down closes any open dashboard on its next request.
VerifiedPass. The threshold is validated against people who can actually answer — verified contacts only — and a shortfall is reported as a fatal blocker naming the fix. Stand-down was walked end to end: an open access plan closed on reload and the recipient was shown a graceful close rather than an expiry error.

UC-05 — Read what happened while they were away

ActorOwner
GoalAfter an emergency, know what was opened, by whom, and that it is shut.
ExpectedA record in plain words — not a hash table.
VerifiedPass. “On 8/12/2026 an emergency was raised, Dr. Alex Chen confirmed it was real, and 1 thing you had set aside was opened. It is closed again now.” Items are listed with timestamps; repeat opens of the same item count once. The append-only chain remains below it, with client-side verification.

UC-06 — Leave

ActorOwner
GoalTake the data out, or close the account entirely.
ExpectedSelf-serve export and deletion. Closing cancels billing, removes the vault, passkeys, emergency codes and outstanding invitations, and releases the owner from any circle they were standing by for — telling those owners rather than silently shrinking their plan.
VerifiedPass. Deletion was exercised against production repeatedly during this session; every fixture teardown used the product’s own deletion path and left zero orphaned rows.

UC-07 — Standing by for somebody

ActorStandby contact
GoalKnow who they are covering and what would be asked of them — without being told.
ExpectedThe reassuring case is the common one: “Nothing is open. There is nothing you need to do today.” A passkey can be added so a new phone does not lock them out. Stepping down is always available, and the owner is told.
VerifiedPass. Passkey enrolment and usernameless sign-in were proven end to end with a virtual authenticator. Stepping down was proven for both reasons — “I can no longer do this” and “I do not know this person” — which record distinctly in the owner’s audit chain.

UC-08 — Accept a place

ActorNamed contact
GoalAccept, using a code the owner gave them.
ExpectedA typed code, never a link that signs you in. Accepting binds an identity and mints a session in one act.
VerifiedPass. Walked repeatedly on production. The emailed link is bare, so a forwarded invitation grants nothing.

UC-09 — Get back in after losing a device

ActorStandby contact, or somebody who never accepted
GoalAct when the usual sign-in is gone.
ExpectedA single-use emergency code. Using it is loud: it burns itself, emails the owner, and drops the person to unverified so the owner is asked to check again.
VerifiedPass. Redeem → session → standby dashboard. Replay refused. A revoked person is refused, and the refusal does not consume their code.

UC-10 — Answer “is this real?”

ActorVerifier
GoalAttest that an emergency is genuine, without an account and without seeing the vault.
ExpectedThree distinct answers — yes, no, I don’t know — with “no” at equal prominence. Scale is shown so proportionality can be judged; contents never are.
VerifiedPass. A verified verifier answers from their dashboard with no token in the URL. An unverified one may still answer; the answer is recorded and audited but does not advance the threshold, and the screen says so rather than thanking them.

UC-11 — Open what was set aside

ActorRecipient
GoalReach what the owner left them, once a release is confirmed.
ExpectedA prioritised plan. Decryption happens in the browser; the server returns a wrapped key and never holds plaintext.
VerifiedPass. Authorisation proven by a gate gradient: refused while armed, refused for an out-of-scope item, and reaching AWS KMS for an in-scope item on a released trigger. A re-arm closes the plan on its next request.

UC-12 to UC-14 — Public surfaces

ActorVisitor
ExpectedAn honest account of what the product does, what it does not, what is stored, and what a standby account is.
VerifiedPass. Terms and Privacy were both rewritten on 2026-08-12 to describe standby accounts, which had been live and taking payments while both documents described a product with one kind of user.
